## 0. What the owner decided (2026-10-31)

- **A cash invoice prints a fiscal receipt** for the cash paid, with the invoice number on it, and a cash-register document („CS") is linked to the invoice.
- **At every cash payment:** when the invoice is issued and paid at the counter, and again whenever a cash payment is recorded on it later.
- **A debit note paid in cash** prints a sale receipt.
- **A credit note refunded in cash** prints a storno of the invoice's receipt. Partial storno is built (#363).
- **A cash refund with no receipt to storno is refused.**
- **Stock moves once, by the invoice.** That was the wording of the option the owner picked. An earlier design note said the opposite (the CS moves the stock, as in R0), so §1 put the choice back to him, and he confirmed the invoice.
- **A device that is not fiscalized prints a text receipt** (2026-11-01). The device decides, never a switch: §6.

## 1. The decision: which document moves the stock

Today, an invoice paid in cash is settled on issue (`syncCashSettlement`, payment origin `cash_on_issue`). Nothing prints. Two models can add the receipt:

| | **A — the invoice is the sale** (picked 2026-10-31) | **B — the cash sale is the sale** (R0's end state) |
|---|---|---|
| Stock and revenue | The invoice, as every invoice does today | A CS made from the invoice's lines. The invoice gets `cashSaleRef`, moves no stock and posts no JE. |
| What the CS is | The receipt: money and fiscal facts, no stock, no revenue | The sale |
| Paid in full at issue | ✓ | ✓ (every reader already counts R0 once) |
| **Paid later** | ✓ a receipt for the payment | ✗ The invoice dispatched and posted at issue. Reaching B means reversing both on the payment day, which moves revenue and COGS from the tax-event date to the payment date. |
| **Paid in parts** | ✓ one receipt per payment | ✗ A payment is not a set of goods, so there are no lines to give the CS |
| Debit note in cash | ✓ | ✗ same problem as a part payment |
| Credit-note refund in cash | ✓ a storno, money only; the credit note returns the goods | ✓ but a storno of the CS returns the goods too, so it collides with the credit note (§3.5 of FR-21: a quantity is corrected once) |

**Decision: A** (owner, 2026-10-31), for every cash payment on an invoice-side document. R0 keeps its own direction: a sale rung at the till first and invoiced afterwards ends as B, and that stays. Each document says which direction it came from: `cashSaleRef` on an R0 invoice, `paidInvoiceId` on a receipt (§2.1).

## 2. Design (model A)

### 2.1 Two new cash-register documents

| | `payment_receipt` | `payment_storno` |
|---|---|---|
| Is | the fiscal receipt of a payment on an invoice or a debit note | the storno receipt of a cash refund on a credit note |
| Links | `register.paidInvoiceId`, plus the payment row's key | `register.refundOf` (the credit note) and `register.storno.original*` (the receipt it reverses: УНП, number, date) |
| Number | the register's series (`CS-…`, D-5), shown in the cash-register journal | the same series |
| Stock | none | none |
| Revenue JE | none: the invoice posted it | none: the credit note posted it |
| Money | `payment.recorded` on the invoice, as today | the refund recorded on the credit note |

**Why new types, not a `cash_sale` with a flag.** Every report that counts sales names its types, so with a new type it never sees the receipt. The worst outcome there is a drawer count that comes up short at the close, where it shows. With a flag, every one of those reports would have to learn it, and any that didn't would count the money twice, which nothing shows. This is FR-21 D-2's argument again.

⚠ That argument covers the readers that name their types, and no others. A reader with no `type` in its filter sees a receipt as one more paid document with a customer on it, and counts the money twice with nothing to show for it. Each of those says so (`NOT_PAYMENT_RECEIPT`), and §3 says what that means for a rollback.

### 2.2 What prints, and when

| Event | Prints | Payment type |
|---|---|---|
| Invoice issued with method cash, at the counter | a `payment_receipt` for the total | cash |
| Cash payment recorded on an issued invoice (`POST /invoices/:id/payment`) | a `payment_receipt` for that amount | cash |
| Debit note issued or paid in cash | a `payment_receipt` (a sale receipt) | cash |
| Credit note refunded in cash | a `payment_storno` citing a receipt that paid the invoice (§2.5) | cash |
| Card at the counter: invoice issued with method card, or a card payment recorded | a `payment_receipt`, by the same rules as cash (owner, Q-2) | card |
| Invoice with method cash on delivery | At issue (owner, Q-2), payment type „НАЛОЖЕН ПЛАТЕЖ" (Eltrade slot R), with the invoice still due until the courier pays | slot R |
| **Any imported document** (Files app, `convertDraftInvoice`, the API import skills) | **never**. Another system printed its receipt. | — |
| Bank transfer, `other` | nothing | — |

**What the receipt says.** A payment that settles the whole invoice at issue prints the invoice's lines, like any sale, followed by a text line „Фактура № …". Any other payment prints one line per VAT rate, „Плащане по фактура № …", with the amount split pro rata. The last payment takes the remainder, so the receipts add up to the invoice exactly, rate by rate. The accountant reviews the printed wording against Н-18 during the go-live preparation (**Q-3**), so it is built as written here and corrected if that review says so.

**The driver gap.** `IFiscalPrinter` could not print text inside a fiscal receipt; `printNonFiscalText` works only on a non-fiscal one. One optional method is added, `printFiscalText(text)`, with a capability flag (`fiscalText`). On Eltrade it is ISL 36h, „PRINTING A FREE FISCAL TEXT" in the protocol v1.1.1: up to 46 bytes, framed in `#`, S1.1 with no fiscal receipt open. ErpNet.FP's ISL drivers send comments with it. It has not run on a physical device. A device without it prints the invoice number as the line's name.

### 2.3 Where it prints

- **Through the issuing user's open session**, on that session's device (D-13). The cash goes into that drawer.
- **No open session, or the device isn't connected:** refused before anything is written, the printer gate's 409 `fiscal_device_not_connected` (#390). Nothing is issued as paid, and no number or УНП is drawn.
- **Orgs where LGR prints nothing:** with the gate's switch off (`settings.cashRegister.requireConnectedDevice: false`), cash invoices behave as today: settled on issue, no receipt. There is one switch, not two (owner, Q-4).
- **Off unless the org opts in (owner, 2026-11-01).** Every path that creates an org writes the switch off, explicitly: `defaultOrgSettings` (registration, OAuth, `POST /auth/orgs`), the demo seed and `import-regal-pz`. An org turns it on when its sales print here. ⚠ This narrows #390's "every org": the gate binds an org that opted in, or that predates this and was not turned off below. There is one switch, so it narrows the till's gate with the invoices'.
- ⚠ **An UNSET value still reads as on.** `regal`'s real till depends on that, and a default flipped under it would stop its gate silently. New orgs get an explicit false instead, and the reading does not change.
- **Existing orgs are a migration:** `0012-printer-gate-off` writes `false` on every org with no active fiscal device whose switch is unset, on every node, and takes only the orgs homed there. An org with an active device keeps what it has, and a value somebody set is theirs. `bun run migrate --dry-run` says what it would do.
- **One org is a named step:** `scripts/printer-gate-off.ts` is for `regal-pz`, which has a device and whose till is still PlusMinus: nothing in the data says that. It refuses `regal`: out of its plan whatever it holds, out of its write's filter, and naming it stops the script.
- ⚠ **R1's deploy order:** on lgrai.app both run BEFORE the new image serves, because `MIGRATIONS_AUTO` is off there and every cash invoice in an org with the gate unset would then need an LGR device. A master runs the migration by itself, before it listens. Each store turns the switch on when it moves to the LGR till.
- **Every refusal of the gate names the switch and where it lives** (Settings → Organization → Cash register), in all four languages and in the API's own message: an org with no printer has no other way out.
- **What the switch does to an org that already has receipts (R2).** Turned off, nothing new prints: a cash invoice settles on issue with no receipt, a refund is recorded with no storno, and the device keeps that sale. What stands stands: a receipt or a storno still freezes its document (§2.7), and its budget is not read again. By decision one switch, so nobody is asked. The one thing it does not get past is a storno held in doubt: it holds its note's cash and card refunds whatever the switch says, because going past it is how a device ends up with two (§7).
- **The one deliberate change where nothing prints (R2).** A refund's entry the wrong way round is wrong in any org, so what reads a refund's direction is not gated: `createPaymentJournalEntry` posts it as money out; the digest's `salesTotal`, `paymentsTotal` and `byMethod` and the summary's "paid in 30 days" read it negative; the form's action says "Refund". Everything else in such an org is what it was: the same refusals and rows, no role, no session, no cap. Prod check 2026-11-02: no credit note in any org carries a payment row and none has a payment entry, so there is no history to repair.

### 2.4 Order of operations

The till's print-once machinery (#155) is reused unchanged: `register.fiscal.state` pending → printed / failed / unknown, `counterBefore`, and the reconciliation of an `unknown`.

1. Check the session, the device, the amount (≤ what is due) and the `Idempotency-Key`.
2. **At issue:** issue the invoice first, because the receipt names its number. It gets its number, dispatches its stock and posts `invoice.posted`, as today.
3. Insert the `payment_receipt` with `fiscal.state: 'pending'`, then print.
4. **Printed:** record the payment row (linked to the receipt), then `payment.recorded`.
5. **Failed:** the receipt is `failed` and no payment is recorded. An invoice issued in step 2 stays issued and unpaid (`sent`, amount due), and the user takes the payment again with the Pay button. Nothing is rolled back and no number is lost.
6. **It may have printed (`unknown`):** nothing is recorded and nothing prints. It is held for a person, who says which, as a dead receipt is (§6, §9 D3).

`syncCashSettlement` keeps settling cash invoices on issue in orgs where LGR prints nothing. In orgs where it prints, the receipt and its payment are the settlement.

### 2.5 Refunds, partial storno, refusals

**A credit note refunded in cash** prints a `payment_storno` for the refund, citing a receipt that paid its invoice:
- for an invoice paid at the counter or later: one of its `payment_receipt`s;
- for an R0 invoice: the till sale's own receipt.
- **Budget:** per receipt and per VAT rate, the stornos citing a receipt never exceed what it printed. A later claim that doesn't fit gives way. (The spec said `_id` order, as R0's invoice claims; §7 says why it is a conditional update instead.)
- **Which receipt:** the newest with enough left. A refund larger than any one receipt's remainder prints one storno per receipt it draws on.
- **Partial:** a refund smaller than the receipt is a partial storno of it. Н-18 allows that, and the device needs no new command.
- **Refused (409 `no_receipt_to_storno`):** a cash refund on a credit note whose invoice has no receipt in LGR to storno. That covers an invoice paid by bank, an imported invoice, and one paid before this FR. The message says to refund by bank.
- **Refused (422):** a refund larger than what the invoice's receipts still hold.
- **Risk:** no credit note has ever been refunded in cash through `POST /invoices/:id/payment`. The `payment.recorded` handler must post a credit note's payment as money OUT (Dr receivable / Cr cash). R2's first test checks this.

**The till's own partial storno (#363)**, for a sale rung at the till:
- `register.stornoState` gains `'partial'`.
- A per-line budget: sold minus what live stornos returned, claimed atomically like R0's claims, and safe under `Idempotency-Key` retries.
- The storno receipt carries only the returned lines and cites the original УНП.
- Stock returns for those lines only, at the cost they left at (`returnStornoStock`).
- Readers need no change: a storno already counts at its own amount (`cash_sale − cash_storno`). The journal's badge says „частично".
- **An invoiced till sale stays refused** (FR-21 §3.5). It is corrected through its invoice's credit note, whose cash refund prints a `payment_storno` citing the sale's receipt.

### 2.6 Who reads what

| Reader | `payment_receipt` | `payment_storno` |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue: sales history, digest KPIs, Reports, exports, ledgers, dashboard | not a sale; the invoice counts | not a refund of revenue; the credit note counts |
| Cash-register journal | listed: number, УНП, amount, the invoice it paid | listed |
| Session: expected cash, close preview, session report (#391), Z-report check | counts: the cash is in the drawer | counts negative |
| Session close JE (FR-21 D-6) | excluded: `payment.recorded` posted the cash | excluded |
| Stock | none | none |
| SUPTO tables that list receipts (18.2–18.5) | listed. **This changes the SUPTO database structure: FR-20.50's declaration rule applies.** R1 is SUPTO version 2 (`config/supto.json`); `declared` stays empty until the owner declares it. | listed |
| Digest "sales with no movement" (FR-11.10) | excluded: no movement is right | excluded |
| Invoice PDF | lists the receipts that paid it (УНП and number), as R0's PDF lists its sale's | — |

### 2.7 Edits and voids

Once a receipt has printed for an invoice, its total can no longer change: `PUT` answers 409 `fiscally_paid` to anything that would move it, and void is refused. A printed receipt cannot change, so the invoice is corrected with a credit or debit note, which prints its own storno or receipt. Every other invoice stays editable in every status, as today. (**Q-5**)

| | Contents | Rollback |

Each release goes through @lazarovski's protocol on #171, and the SUPTO docs change in the PR that changes the structure (R1).

**R1's rollback.** With no `payment_receipt` in the database, redeploying the previous image is all it takes. Once one exists it is not clean, and a receipt on a device cannot be taken back:

- **The previous image counts every receipt again.** Its readers with no `type` have no `NOT_PAYMENT_RECEIPT`: `GET /invoices`, the invoice summary (paid in 30 days), the digest's KPIs, `byMethod` and `paymentsTotal`, the contact ledger with its cost, `/stats/*` and the contact's summary. A cash invoice of 24.00 reads as 48.00 paid, and a receipt printed whole adds its products to the customer's ledger and cost. Nothing marks those figures wrong.
- **It loses the link.** On `payment.recorded` it writes the `payments` array back through its own schema, which has no `receiptId`. A payment recorded under it no longer points at its receipt after a roll forward.
- **It does not know a hold or a claim.** A receipt in doubt reads as a paid document, and its money as neither booked nor held. A storno in doubt reads as a refund nobody made, and its sale as reversed.
- **It settles on an edit.** The old `/send` and `PUT` call `syncCashSettlement` with no gate: the gate was the till's alone. Any edit of an unpaid cash or card invoice, one whose receipt failed for instance, adds a `cash_on_issue` row for what is outstanding: money nobody took, with no receipt, and on a part-paid one the remainder.
- **It has no freeze (§2.7).** A receipted invoice's total can move, and a `sent` cash-on-delivery invoice with a printed receipt can be voided.
- **Its SUPTO tables lose the receipts.** Its `tillRows` loads none, so 18.1, 18.2, 18.3 and 18.5 lose every one the device holds.
- **Its till and its invoices print as they did:** no receipt for a cash invoice, which is a sale the device never saw if the org already opted in.

So a rollback needs, in this order: settle every receipt and storno in doubt; finish the sessions that hold them; tell people not to edit a cash or card invoice under it; say which reports are wrong, SUPTO's 18.1, 18.2, 18.3 and 18.5 among them, and until when. ⚠ Do not turn the printer gate off for it. It changes nothing for invoices, because the old `/send` and `PUT` never read it. It takes the gate off the till, which is what `scripts/printer-gate-off.ts` warns about, and those orgs stay off after the roll forward until each opts in again. The way back is a roll forward, not a repair: this release's readers are right about everything the old image wrote, but for the `receiptId` of a payment it recorded and the rows an edit added. Prefer fixing forward.

**R2's rollback.** To R1, or to the image before it, everything said above holds of a `payment_storno` too, and more: an R1 image has the type in no enum and in no reader, so each reader with no `type` reads a storno as one more paid document with a customer on it, and `NOT_PAYMENT_RECEIPT` there does not name it. A credit note in cash issued under R1 prints nothing and stays due, and a refund on one records money with no storno: the device keeps the sale. `register.refundClaims` on a receipt is ignored, so a storno R1 cannot see leaves its budget looking whole. R1's `payment.recorded` posts a credit note's refund as money IN, the wrong way round. So roll back only with no refund printed, or fix forward.

**Two versions at once (R2).** What is above speaks of an image going back. Three things put an older one beside this one. During a roll (`maxSurge: 1`), a refund reaching the old pod, directly or by a hop to a device it still holds, records money with no storno and posts it as money in, and a close there does not wait for a held storno: bounded by the length of the roll, and no worse than before R2. On a move of an org with refunds to a master not yet on R2, nothing checks the version (`NODE_MIN_MASTER_VERSION` defaults to `0.1.0`), and that master reads the stornos as described above. On a fenced import to a hub older than the master, R1's `bookingOf` reads a `payment_storno` as a sale, with its row as money in: unusual, since a hub normally leads. The mirror itself is safe: raw writes, no type logic.

## 6. R1 as built (2026-10-31)

Where the spec left a choice, what was chosen:

- **What proves a print (the owner's rulings, 2026-11-01).** In a document's turn on the device the counter is evidence; out of one it is not, because the device is shared, and that goes for every document, a sale and a storno too. A print that throws in its turn is judged by `verdictOfFailedPrint`. First, what the driver saw: `cancelFiscalReceipt` says `voided` or `finished`, or nothing (`AbandonOutcome`). Voided is `failed` at every stage, whatever the counter reads. Finished is the driver's fallback closing it: past the money, counter +1 is `printed`, and otherwise `unknown`, never `failed`, but for `opening`: nothing of this document is on the device yet, so what closed there is an earlier document's, and it is `failed` (§7). With no word from the driver: before the money it is `failed`, whatever the counter says; past it, counter +1 is `printed`, unmoved and idle is `failed`, and anything else is `unknown`, because a real receipt voided and printed again is two fiscal receipts for one payment. What an earlier document left open is cleared before the counter is read, in the same turn. A resumed document is proved not printed (`provedNotPrinted`), or it is not printed again: a sale goes `unknown`, and a receipt or a storno is held.
- ⚠ **`unknown` means a person must look at the device.** Nothing reads it as `failed`, and nothing reads it as `printed`. A sale's money stands either way (D-4). A receipt's and a storno's never do, whichever turn the print was in: each is held for a person (§9 D3).
- ⚠ **The driver may say voided only if it saw a receipt open before it cancelled.** After a close whose answer was lost nothing is open, 3Ch does nothing and the status reads not open, and reading that as voided would book a real receipt as failed and print it again. So it reads the open flag first. Not open, or a flag it cannot read, means it says neither, and the counter decides. A fallback that loses its answer says finished: it may have closed.
- ⚠ **A driver's word speaks only for this turn, and only for a receipt this document opened.** `cancelFiscalReceipt` reads `this.receipt` before it clears it, and says nothing when that is null, though it clears the device all the same. A leftover the clear could not close refuses this document's 90h, and read as this document's, its fallback finishing the leftover booked a payment the device never saw. A 90h whose answer was lost leaves `this.receipt` null too: what it opened has no lines and no money, so it is voided, and the verdict before the money is `failed`, which is what happened. A new document's open forgets whatever an earlier one left.
- **Whether a real device's 71h numbers a voided receipt is still not known, and no longer matters where the driver saw the void.** The simulator's 3Ch does not move it, and neither the protocol nor a physical device has said. A driver that says nothing, Tremol and Datecs, has no 3Ch to ask about.
- **A storno that may have printed is held, not voided** (`holdInDoubt`). Only `failed` voids it and gives the sale back. An `unknown` one keeps its claim on the sale, books no refund, no stock and no session totals, and answers 409 `storno_in_doubt`: it may have printed, nothing was reversed, check the device and settle it, do not press again. Every later press says the same and prints nothing. Voided, the next press printed a second fiscal storno. The claim is `register.stornoState: 'full'` on the sale: it has no lease of its own, and a storno proved not to have printed, or settled so, gives it back. With no printer on a resume, one that never started is `failed`, and one that started is held. A dead storno is found by its lapsed lease, as a receipt is, by the sale's next storno and by its session's close, and it holds the close. It counts nowhere until it is settled: a storno `pending`, `unknown`, or `failed` and not yet voided (a settle "not printed" that stopped before its void) is left out of every reader that sums, and out of SUPTO's 18.2 and 18.4, and its sale out of 18.5. One list, `UNPROVED_STORNO_STATES`, read by `stornoUnproved`, `NOT_UNPROVED_STORNO`, the digest, 18.5 and a fenced import's `bookingOf`. A storno checks the session in its turn, as a receipt does.
- **A claim whose storno was never written is the one thing that lapses** (`releaseOrphanClaims`). The process died between `claimForStorno` and the insert: nothing printed and nothing was booked, but the journal read it as stornoed with nothing related, the next press was refused and 18.5 listed a cancellation that never happened. An id in `register.stornoedBy` with no document, on a claim older than `LEASE_MS` (`register.cancelledAt`), is pulled in one conditional write, id by id, by the storno door before it reads the sale and by the close for its session's sales, because R3 keeps several ids there and a live insert is milliseconds behind its claim. A request that stalled that long wakes with no claim, and prints nothing: it is voided with its number (`lostItsClaim`). Beyond what was ruled: younger than that a press answers 409 `storno_in_progress`, and past it the sale's page reads as not stornoed, because a sale whose session has closed is only reached by a press from that page.
- **A sale that went `unknown` is not refunded with no storno on the device.** The storno door refuses a fiscal sale in doubt before the claim: 409 `sale_receipt_in_doubt`. A sale still running, fiscal or text, answers 409 `sale_in_progress`: nothing of it is booked, so a storno refunded money that never landed, and the keyed retry then booked it and moved its stock with nothing to bring that back. Prod holds no such document, so no data step is needed.
- ⚠ **A sale whose request died is finished before anything reads its session's figures or reverses it** (`saleProgress`, `finishDeadSale`, `finishSale`). Every reader of the rows counts it and its session does not, so the preview said one thing and the close another, the drawer read as over by exactly that, and the entry booked it as cash and again as over. Dead means no `completedAt`, not `register.legacy`, and a lapsed lease, or with no lease a sale of a session older than `LEASE_MS`. It has to be "no `completedAt`", not "`pending`": a sale can stop after its print and before its totals. It prints nothing: a receipt still `pending` goes `unknown`, and its money stands (D-4). Its stock takes the till's rule, `allowNegative`, because the goods have left.
  - **The close** finishes it before it reads `expected`, and what is left holds the close (`receipts_unfinished`, `kind: 'sale'`): a live sale, or one whose finish threw. The preview writes nothing, so it adds what that would book (`saleIncrements`), names them (`deadSales`), and the dialog says they count and to check the device.
  - **The storno door** finishes it before `stornoRefusal` reads the sale, so a text sale goes through with its money booked, and a fiscal one answers `sale_receipt_in_doubt` until a person says. An imported day is refused as one, and left.
  - **A joined sale's transfers are on the document** (`register.transfersToConfirm`; `pendingTransferIds` there means already confirmed), so whatever finishes a sale confirms them first. They lived only in the request, and a sale settled after its request died dispatched from a warehouse that never received them. A confirm that fails there sets `stockError` and never fails the settle.
  - **Beyond what was ruled:** a sale of a session with no key takes a lease too. With none `keepLease` has nothing to renew, so one waiting its turn behind a slow Z read as dead by its age alone.
  - An old sale with no session, no lease and no `completedAt` is not one of these: it may be from before `completedAt` existed, and running its steps would dispatch it again.
- **The settle door takes all three** (`settle.service.ts`, `POST /cash-register/documents/:id/settle`, the same rights as a storno). A storno: printed runs the rest of it with no device, not printed voids it with its number and gives the sale back. A sale: its money stands either way. Printed needs the receipt number off the slip, because `printStorno` cites `register.storno.originalReceiptNumber`, copied from it, and the drivers refuse a storno with no digits there before anything is sent. It is kept as typed. Not printed makes it `failed`, whose storno prints nothing. An `unknown` sale is claimed only with its lease free, because it goes `unknown` before its totals and its stock, and a settle landing there dispatched it twice: the loser hears 409 `sale_in_progress`, and a lease left by a crash lapses into what a settle can finish. A receipt or storno that arrived from a site under its placeholder is numbered as it is settled printed, never booked under `~pending:`.
- ⚠ **A person's word is taken only for what only a person can know. What the device can still change, or can answer, it is asked first** (the third read, 2026-11-02). Nothing is final while the device holds a receipt open. The next document clears it: voided if it took no money, finished in cash if it did, which prints it. Settled "not printed" first, that is a fiscal document nobody booked and a second on the next press, and a half-printed slip with no money on the device reads as printed, and the next clear voids it. So for a fiscal document both answers get past the device it printed on, read by `register.fiscalDeviceNumber` (`settleDevice`):
  - **Active and here:** asked, in its turn. Open: 409 `receipt_open_on_device`, until an X report clears it. Busy: 409 `device_busy`. A read that failed, or one unanswered inside the request's 30 s: `receipt_device_away`. Only a driver with no way to ask (no `isReceiptOpen`: Tremol, Datecs) leaves it to the person. Beyond what was ruled, the door waits 10 s for the turn and 20 s for the answer, because someone is looking at the page: a device switched off with its tab open takes a driver 90 s to give up on, and an answer past the cut reaches nobody.
  - **Active and not here:** 409 `receipt_device_away`, both answers, nothing written. Held by another pod no hop reached: 503 `fiscal_holder_unreachable`.
  - **No row, or `isActive: false`:** the person's word stands. That is the way out for a device gone for good (`DELETE /fiscal/device/:id`, with the session still open), so nothing is stuck. A row with no such field has not been deactivated.
  - A text document is none of this: the device holds no money on one.

  It is not `documentContext`, which turns every refusal into "no device", 503s included, and needs a session. It is not `getFiscalContext`, where a device away whose row reads `error` throws. A device connected while its row reads `error` is asked, and a document on a closed session, or on none, reaches its device. The route is the document's own device (`deviceFromSettledDocument`), because a warehouse can hold two; `deviceFromJournalDocument` stays for the storno.
- **A number typed off the slip is read against the device, and can be corrected.** Digits. Not above the device's last number, read in the turn the open flag was read in (422 `receipt_number_off_device`): a device that cannot say skips this, and so does one whose counter has gone back since this sale read it (`counterBefore`, beyond what was ruled), because its fiscal memory was replaced and it speaks for another series. Not one the same device gave another document within 31 days either side of this sale, compared as a number, whatever padding is stored or typed (422 `receipt_number_taken`): by org and device number, never the session, because two sessions on one device is normal.
  - ⚠ **Both are questions, not walls** (the fourth read). The taken check rests on the same unproved series the lower bound was refused over. If a slip's series is the fiscal one, its numbers are ones the document counter passed days ago, usually under another receipt, and a real slip reads as taken with nothing to lift it but a false citation. So the answer names what it collided with, and the same request with `confirmNumber: true`, on the door's own rights, goes through, for a settle and for a correction. The trail records that it was confirmed and what over (`register.settled.confirmedOver`). The page shows the message and offers "the slip says this number". Digits are never confirmable, nor is `receipt_number_cited`, which protects a storno already on the device.
  - ⚠ **No lower bound.** `getLastReceiptNumber` and the stored number are both 71h, but nobody has held a real slip against 71h, and if the slip's series is the fiscal one it runs below the device's document counter. A lower bound there would refuse a real sale with no way out.
  - **Correcting one:** the same door and rights, `{ printed: true, receiptNumber }` on a sale settled printed with a typed number, under the same checks, audited as `correct_receipt_number`, all three stored fields rewritten. Once a storno cites it, or (beyond what was ruled) an invoice for the sale carries it, printed: 409 `receipt_number_cited`. A storno voided since cites nothing. `claimForStorno` answers the sale as claimed and the storno is written from that, so of a correction and a claim one gives way. A device's number was never typed and is not corrected.
- **A receipt in doubt.** One whose request died and never reached the device, or whose device confirms the counter unmoved and nothing open, is given up. Any other is held, and so is one whose verdict in its own turn is `unknown`: `unknown` + `inDoubt`, no money booked, nothing printed, its hold kept. A person who checked the device settles it, yes or no, in the journal (`POST /cash-register/documents/:id/settle`, the storno's roles, audited). What they said is durable from the claim, and the claim leaves a lease, so a settle that stops halfway is finished by the next touch of the document or by the close, with no device. A second `/settle` saying the same finishes it; one saying the opposite is refused, 409 `receipt_already_settled`.
- **A printed verdict keeps the error.** A driver that finishes a part-paid receipt tenders the rest in cash, whatever LGR recorded, so the receipt carries `receiptError` saying the device's payment types may differ. `printed` stands.
- **The freeze and a void are claims, not checks.** `voidingUntil` is the one claim on a document's money, taken by a void and by an attach to a cash sale, honoured in `fitsDue`, which both payment doors share. `/send` and `/void` respect each other's claim. A money-moving `PUT` is a conditional write: no hold, the `amountPaid` it read, no claim. The hold alone would not do, because a receipt that finished between the read and the write has let go of it. `syncCashSettlement`'s own write carries the same condition: lost, it reads again and runs the rule once more, and lost again it leaves the document as the payments left it. Attach reads what got in before its claim, a hand payment as well as a receipt, because its last step replaces the rows, and pays from the document as claimed. A write that lost retires no journal entry: they go once it has landed, or a row whose entry was voided drops out of the ledger with the document still saying it was paid.
- **A draft has the same one mechanism** (`unclaimedDraft`). Its `PUT` and `DELETE` are conditional on still being a draft with no issue under way and no claim, and answer 409 with who holds it, or `document_changed`. `/send` decides from what it read, so its claim is a compare-and-set on `updatedAt`: a draft changed between the read and the claim answers 409 `document_changed`, with nothing moved and no number drawn. `/receive` honours it as well. A `DELETE` takes the claim first (`voidingUntil`), then gives a bill's receipts back, then deletes: the order FR-22 needs, because the bill is the only record of the supplier it wrote on them, and a delete that loses has given nothing back. A link to goods receipts honours it too: a link inside a bill's `/send` replaced the lines it was issuing.
- ⚠ **The issue holds its claim until the receipt does.** The compare-and-set covers a draft up to the issue's write, and until the receipt document exists nothing else stands on the document: an edit in that gap rang 36 for a receipt of 24, and bank to cash settled with no receipt. So when `/send` will print, that write keeps `issue.leaseUntil` and records `issue.tender`, what it decided to take, and `printAtIssue` lets go in a `finally`; a crash lets it lapse. What prints nothing keeps no lease. A money-moving `PUT` honours it, 409 `issue_in_progress`. A lapsed lease or a retry holds no claim, so the last check before the device compares the document with `issue.tender`: moved, nothing prints, the document stands issued and unpaid, and `receiptError` with `receiptCode: 'issue_tender_moved'` says to take the payment with Pay, or to print a delivery receipt on its page. Paid since, it says nothing. `/send`'s last `syncCashSettlement` gets `settles` as `PUT` computes it (`ruleSettles`), but for the re-issue of a number issued twice, which settles as it is issued and only then (FR-20.51).
- **One turn per device** (`fiscal/device-turn.ts`), in the process holding it, from the counter read to the close, for a sale, a storno, a receipt and every report. It is the "two sales on one driver" risk, which sending back-office payments to the till's device widened. A wait past `TURN_WAIT_MS`, longer than the slowest report, answers 409 `device_busy`.
- **The amount is held on the invoice** (`receiptHolds`) while a receipt prints, in one atomic update with the check that it fits. The budget of §2.5 is taken in `_id` order because a sale is never written to; an invoice is written to anyway, and the hold also stops a payment by bank taking the same money during the print.
- **A request that died is finished by whoever next touches the document, or by the session's close.** That one prints nothing. A keyed retry of the same request may print, on the device it started on, when the proof above holds. A lease lapsed is not a dead request by itself: `keepLease` renews it while a print waits or runs.
- **A session cannot close over an unfinished receipt, storno or sale.** It finishes what it can prove and refuses the rest with 409 `receipts_unfinished`, naming them, before any Z-report. A closed session is never incremented.
- ⚠ **The preview says what the close will book, by the function it books with** (`bookedOnItsSession`, the fourth read). The steps of a sale, a receipt and a storno read that one function, and so does the preview, so neither can drift. What the close finishes first is the query the close runs (`interruptedDocuments`). The count is prefilled from the preview, and a receipt or storno that stopped after its print used to close 24 short or 12 over on it. A document that stopped is named as one the close will finish, and one nothing can prove stops that close. Only what the close will refuse over reads as "cannot close": one still running, and one held for a person. A dead sale's line says to settle it only when a person can: a text sale, or one that printed, has nothing to say.
- ⚠ **A session takes nothing new unless it is `open`** (the fourth read). A sale let in during the close's Z printed after it: on the next day's Z, its money in no drawer, while the closed session's report and entry counted it. A text sale's storno moved `totalReturns` on a session whose close had already stored what it expected.
  - **The door:** a new sale and a new till storno answer 409 `session_closing`. A keyed retry is answered above that, so a sale made before the close replays through all of it. A session back to `open` after a dead close sells again.
  - **The look that holds:** a document looks at its session once it is written, and the close looks at the session's documents once it has claimed it, so one of the two sees the other. Read as open, the close finds it and refuses. Read as not, it gives way. A sale looks with no number or УНП yet on a keyed sale, and again in its turn on the device. A receipt and a fiscal storno look in their turn. With nothing to print there is no turn, and a sale or a storno looks once it is written, so what was built for no device is both: a close landing before its insert makes it give way, and one landing after makes the close refuse. One that finishes between the claim and the second look is found by neither, so the close takes `expected` after that look.
  - **What giving way is:** a sale is removed, as any sale that never reached the device or the drawer, and the cashier reads the refusal with the basket still on the screen. ⚠ Removed, not voided: a sale is flipped to `cancelled` by its storno alone, and a voided one would count in every reader of the rows. A keyed sale that gives way at its first look has lost nothing. One that gives way in its turn leaves a gap in its number, and that takes its turn landing in the milliseconds between a claim and its release. A storno is voided with its number, and the sale given back.
  - **A sale whose request died is no new sale:** it is finished whatever the session says, because its money stands.
  - **With no answer the close dialog says nobody knows** (`isOutcomeUnknown`). A Z runs longer than the 30 s a request is let wait, and "still open" sent the next sale into a session whose day had been counted. It says the close may be running, not to sell, to check the device, and that Retry returns that close and never prints a second Z.
- ⚠ **A close that dies in its Z goes back to `open`, and says so.** The claim sets `closeLeaseUntil`: `CLOSE_LEASE_MS = TURN_WAIT_MS + REPORT_MAX_MS`, 330 s for the device's turn plus (30 s + 300 s) × 3 for the status read and the report with their retransmits, 22 minutes. A lapsed one, or one with no lease whose `updatedAt` is older than that, goes back on the next close by someone who may close it, with `fiscalError` set and its key cleared, and both of two presses answer 409 `close_interrupted`: the Z may have printed, so check the device. Printed: close without a fiscal report (a manager). Not printed: close again. Beyond what was ruled, every write of a close from its claim on is conditional on what it holds, so one that comes back past its lease undoes nothing, and the sessions list offers the close for a session stuck there, because the terminal offers only an open one.
- **The link is `payments[].receiptId`.** Other paths still rewrite the array through the repository, which gives every row a new `_id`. `payment.recorded` no longer does: it stamps in place, on `receiptId` or on the `paymentId` the event names, because the array it read is stale by then and writing it back lost a receipt's row while `amountPaid` kept the money.
- **SUPTO 18.1 lists the receipt too**, beside the §2.6 list of 18.2–18.5. It lists every receipt under its УНП, the invoice is in none of those tables, and 18.1 and 18.2 must agree. 18.4 has nothing to list until R2's `payment_storno`. 18.5 lists a failed receipt.
- **A whole payment prints per rate when its lines would not add up.** A device totals rounded VAT-inclusive prices; 1000 × 0.1234 net is 148.08 on the invoice and 150.00 on the device. The line is then named „Фактура № …", as on a device with no text line.
- **A debit note names itself:** „Дебитно известие № …", „Плащане по дебитно известие № …".
- **A cash payment on an imported invoice prints nothing and is recorded**, as §2.2's "never" says. That cash is in no session.
- **A later payment on a COD invoice prints nothing**, by any method, once its delivery receipt has printed: that took all that was owed.
- **A COD receipt that fails at issue prints on its own** (`POST /invoices/:id/receipt`), for what is still owed. §2.4's "the user takes the payment again" cannot apply: the money is the courier's. Until it has printed `/payments` refuses the courier's money (409 `delivery_receipt_owed`), so the sale cannot end with no receipt or part of one; a bank or card payment may go first. The invoice PDF lists a COD receipt only once the invoice is paid. Whether an invoice owes one is a fact (`deliveryReceiptOwed`), written in the issue's own write, because an issue can leave no receipt document at all, and by an edit that moves an issued invoice with no live receipt onto cash on delivery, in an org that prints: with nothing owed the page offered no button and the courier's money printed nothing. A note never carries it, because on cash on delivery only an invoice prints (`printsAtCounter`). `/receipt` waits for an issue under way (409 `issue_in_progress`), and the issue's last check asks again, because a lapsed lease holds no claim and a second delivery receipt books the sale twice.
- **The doors declare their device (FR-24.50).** `/send`, `/payments` and `/receipt` follow the open session of whoever issues or pays, so on two pods they run where that session's printer is. A bank transfer by someone with a session runs there too: a hop, no more, and telling it apart would mean deciding what prints twice. `PUT` and void reach a device only to finish a dead receipt, and follow that receipt's own device.
- **"Held elsewhere" never reads as "offline" (FR-24.52).** With the holder unreachable the doors answer 503 `fiscal_holder_unreachable`, and a request that reached the wrong pod with no hop to take answers 503 from `getFiscalContext`. Once a crashed pod's row has lapsed the device is offline, and the printer gate refuses (409 `fiscal_device_not_connected`). In all three nothing is written, printed or booked. The two-pod lane proves the first and the third across real processes.
- **An unreachable holder keeps the `Idempotency-Key`** (FR-24.51). A hop's connection can die mid-print, so `fiscal_holder_unreachable` is an unknown outcome to the client: pressing again sends the same key and finds what the first attempt did, where a new key could print a second receipt for a part payment.
- **The re-issue of a number issued twice prints nothing** (FR-20.51). It copies the invoice's method, and its money was taken under the number it replaces: a second receipt would book the sale on the device twice, and with no session it would refuse a legal correction.
- **A payment on a draft is refused** where LGR prints (422 `document_not_issued`): a receipt names a number.
- **A document in another currency than the session is refused** (422 `receipt_currency`). The device prints in one currency.
- **A rate with a negative total is refused** (422 `receipt_not_printable`). A device takes no negative line.
- **Imported means §2.2's list, by `ocr_import` on the first status entry.** The `ocr-import` tag is copied by „Копирай", and `importedFrom` is LGR moving its own document, which prints like any other.
- **An edit keeps the settlement rule where no receipt can speak:** an imported invoice, and the rule's own row under a method the invoice no longer says.
- **`/void` claims the document before it moves stock** (`voidingUntil`), against the hold a receipt takes, so of a void and a payment one gives way.
- **A receipt is dated as it prints**, in its turn, whatever the form says and however long it waited, so the device, the session and the row say one day. One that cannot print keeps the date it had.
- **Where LGR prints, a cash or card header on an unpaid invoice, debit note or credit note is no money** (`headerIsNoMoney`). A credit note's money is its refund row, which R2 adds. Until then a cash credit note's open part goes to „По банка" or `unpaid`, never В брой, so a credit note against a receipted invoice reads no refund, as nothing was refunded, while the device holds the receipt. An org with the switch off behaves as it always did, a debit note issued in cash included. Sales history keeps the row's total, because it is issued revenue, and its summary gains `unpaid`, so total = cash + card + other + unpaid. The digest skips the header. The sales register puts the open part in „По банка" (the operator's rule of 2026-10-15: the columns partition Общо, and „По банка" takes anything unpaid), never В брой or Карта, and cash on delivery keeps its own column.
- **A `/send` that will not issue cancels the draft transfers drawn up for it:** not a draft, a frozen series, `document_changed`, `document_claimed`, a refused print. Never on `issue_in_progress`, where the holder may confirm those same ids, nor on the overlap question, whose answer sends them again, nor any id the document already carries. Each retry from the dialog draws new ones, and a stale draft moves goods if somebody confirms it.
- **On a Tremol the number stays in the line's name.** The driver cuts a name at 34 characters and keeps no free text, so the wording shortens to fit (`lineNameMax`); `unknown` is never stored as a receipt number, and a storno of a sale with no number stays fiscal.
- **Under an expired licence "started before" is the local date.** A till sale is refused in a session opened on or after `restrictedFrom`, and one already open keeps selling, as FR-20.55 promised. A debit note written since prints no receipt: its lines are whatever it was given, so that receipt would be a new sale on the device. It can still be issued on a bank transfer.
- **The doors follow a dead receipt's own device** (`register.fiscalDeviceNumber`), never its warehouse: a warehouse can hold two.
- **A device's turn lives in the process holding it, so a move between pods is what can interrupt one.** A graceful stop finishes the queue before it closes the bridges, within `LGR_STOP_WAIT_MS`. A request that waits past the server's 30 s idle limit loses its connection and keeps its place; the `Idempotency-Key` finds what it did. After a crash the receipt's lease (120 s) outlives the presence row (20 s), so for up to two minutes the document answers "printing" on the pod that took the device over, and only then is the receipt proved or held. Nothing prints twice in that window.

### A device that is not fiscalized (owner, 2026-11-01)

The owner's only device is not fiscalized: it refuses a fiscal receipt, so a cash invoice would have answered issued, unpaid and in error on the one device he has.

**The device decides, never a switch.** A receipt is `mode: 'text'`, `isFiscal: false` only when the device it will print on reports `fiscalized === false` in its own status. `undefined` or `true` is fiscal, exactly as before, so a device that does not report it (a Tremol today) never gets text, and a fiscalized device can never print it. `receiptMode` is the one function, and R2's `payment_storno` must use it too.

⚠ **This rests on one reading nobody has seen on a fiscalized unit.** `fiscalized` is status bit S5.3, which the protocol says reads 1 on a fiscalized device. Only the unset side has been seen in the field. If the premise is wrong the failure is silent: every receipt prints as text with its money booked, and the device's fiscal memory holds none of it. **So it is a step: read the status of the first fiscalized Eltrade on the device page before it sells** (Invoicing → Configuration → Devices → the device → Reports → Device Status → Read Status), and it must read Fiscalized: Yes. A wrong reading is visible: the device page shows it, the bridge says it on connecting, and a terminal on such a device carries a chip saying receipts print as text (`terminal-prints-text`). On two pods a terminal whose list is answered by the pod not holding the device keeps the last word it heard, and until one of them has heard the device it shows nothing.

- Read fresh, in the document's turn on the device, with no cache: a unit fiscalized by service while connected goes fiscal on its next receipt.
- A status read that fails is never "not fiscalized". The receipt takes the path of a device that went away: `failed` if it never reached the device, held if it did.
- No request field, org setting or screen selects text. The till's „Нефискален" switch is gone: since the prod sweep a till sale follows this rule too (§9, D1).
- It carries the same words: the lines plus „Фактура № …", or „Плащане по фактура № …" per rate, the wrapped number never cut, the method's name, and the non-fiscal banner. A device that prints no text fails with a message for the back office.
- Money behaves as with a fiscal receipt: printed means payment row and `payment.recorded`. Session totals and the session report count it, as a text receipt.

**Where the till's text sale settles it, it follows that:** no УНП. Listed in 18.1–18.3, with nothing fiscal on the row. On no device witness, in no receipt range, on no Z. No operator code is needed: the door asks the device for someone who has none, and refuses a fiscal receipt as before.

**Where it does not, what was picked, so that it cannot lose money or print twice:**

- **A text print that fails is `failed`,** at any point, with no `verdictOfFailedPrint`. A device holds no money on a text receipt, so there is nothing to be `unknown` about. The receipt is voided with its CS number, which 18.5 accounts for, no payment is recorded, and the next attempt is a new receipt. If the slip did come out, a second one prints: it has no legal weight, and the money is recorded once.
- **A request that died after it reached the device is held for a person,** as a fiscal one is. Text keeps no counter, so nothing proves it, and what is in doubt is the money, not the slip. A keyed retry prints the slip again, as the till does: the same person is at the counter.
- **§2.7 holds.** A text receipt freezes its document as a fiscal one does, because its money is on the document, the session and the slip. `fiscally_paid` says "a receipt". A credit note corrects it, and R2 decides what its refund prints.
- **The unfinished-receipt rule holds at a close,** unchanged.
- **A receipt that started fiscally stays fiscal,** whatever the device says now: what it may have left there is `printOnce`'s to judge. One that started as text is asked again, so a fiscalized device never prints text. On a device fiscalized since it stays text on the record and is held: a person who says it printed books a text receipt, with no УНП and no fiscal receipt on the PDF. A new attempt, or one decided as text that never started, prints as the device says now.
- **The invoice PDF does not list it.** „Платена с касов бон" would say a fiscal receipt exists, as an invoice for a text sale names none either. The document form lists it, because it took the money.

## 7. R2 as built (2026-11-01)

A credit note refunded in cash or by card prints a `payment_storno`, through the steps of §6: hold, number, in its turn how it prints and its УНП, print once, session totals, payment, complete. Where the spec left a choice, what was chosen:

- **Two doors, mirroring the invoice.** `/send`: a credit note issued in cash or by card gives back its total. `/payments`: a refund recorded later, whole or in parts. All of R1's checks apply. Refused at issue, nothing is issued and no number is drawn. A print that fails at issue leaves it issued, its goods back, and nothing refunded; the Refund action gives it. A session that is not `open` takes no refund: 409 `session_closing` at the door, a keyed retry answered above that, as R1's sale (§6). Once written the storno looks again, before it holds anything or draws a number, and gives way leaving no document; in its turn on the device it looks once more. So a close finds it, or it finds the close.
- **The budget is one conditional update on the cited document, per VAT rate (`refund-budget.ts`), not `_id` order.** This is the one place the spec is overruled, and the coordinator's rule for that case applies: take what cannot lose money. A claim that writes and then reads knows only the claims written before its read, and `_id` says nothing about when one was written; two pods mint them independently within a second. So a claim with a later `_id` that read first goes ahead, a claim with an earlier one written after it reads "I am first" and goes ahead too, and one receipt is refunded twice. R0's `overClaimed` has this hole, and there it costs quantities, not money on a device. Here the claim sits on the receipt (`register.refundClaims`: storno, rate, cents), inside one `findOneAndUpdate` with the check that it fits, as a payment's hold is in `fitsDue`. A later claim that does not fit gives way, and later means it reached the document later.
- **The hold and the claim are both taken before a number is drawn,** so a refund that gives way leaves no gap in the series. A storno that does not print gives both back, and one in doubt keeps both.
- **R3 feeds the same budget.** A partial storno at the till takes what it returns per rate with `takeBudget` on its sale, and gives it back with `giveBackBudget`. A refund citing that sale then counts what its stornos gave back, and the other way round. Until R3 a storno is the whole sale: a sale a full storno reversed holds nothing, and a sale a refund took from cannot be stornoed (409 `cash_sale_refunded`). Of the two at once one gives way, each asking of the one document what the other wrote.
- **A storno prints on the device that printed its receipt.** The driver names its own fiscal memory on the storno (`S,<FM>,…`), and a device refuses a УНП that is not its own. So a session on another device is refused, 409 `receipt_on_other_device`, naming it and what a person can do: connect it in this warehouse, refund from a session in its own, or by bank. A refund that fits only with what another device printed answers 422, with what this one holds. With two connected in one warehouse, which is what replacing one looks like, the door asks for the receipt's by number (`connectedInWarehouse`): the device of what still holds money, newest first, as a refund draws, so a person who typed a part can type the rest. "Connected" is this process's word: a device another pod holds is not asked for, and the door refuses as above.

- **Text.** The device decides, with `receiptMode`, in the storno's turn. The door asks the device, and a device that changes between the door and the turn gives up with nothing printed, its hold and claim back.
- **The first test was the risk §2.5 names, and it was real.** `createPaymentJournalEntry` booked every outgoing document's payment Dr cash / Cr receivable. A credit note's is money OUT (Dr receivable / Cr cash), and a supplier's credit note the reverse of a bill's. A credit note's cash or card header is no money where LGR prints (`headerIsNoMoney`), until its refund row says so.

That export's per-method footer now carries a refund's row the same way, so cash there reads the money in plus the money out.

## 8. R3 as built (2026-11-02)

A customer brings back one of three articles. `POST …/storno`, at the till's route and the journal's, takes `lines: [{ lineId, quantity }]`; without it, it means all that is left, so a whole storno is the same request it was. Where the spec left a choice, what was chosen:

- **One claim, in one update** (`claimForStorno`). `register.stornoClaims` (storno, line, thousandths of a unit, cents, its own `at`) and R2's `register.refundClaims` (storno, rate, cents) are written together, with `stornoedBy` and the state, inside one `findOneAndUpdate` whose filter holds both checks: each line as the plan read it, and each rate within what the receipt still holds (`fitsBudget`, R2's). Two budgets would be two updates, and a crash or a race between them is a claim on one and not the other. There is one budget for money, and a till storno, whole or in part, takes from it as a refund does.
- **Compare-and-set on what it read, not `$lte`** (`asPlanRead`). A part's money is read off its line's running total, and the state it writes depends on the other lines: two stornos of two lines at once, each reading the other's as untouched, would each write `partial` with the sale all back. A claim that loses reads again and plans again, up to five times: what it needs may be gone (`storno_exceeds_left`), or it may still fit. ⚠ One exception: all of a sale nothing had touched. Lost, it answers 409 `storno_in_progress` as it always did, and never goes on as "what is left": the person asked for all of it and is told someone else got there.
- **The money adds up by construction.** A line's worth is its share of what the sale printed at its rate (`saleLines`, R1's `apportion`), so the lines come to the receipt. A part is what the line's running total comes to once it is back, less what the parts standing on the line took (`config/pos-totals#partOfLine`, `stornoClaims[].cents`), so the parts come to the line in whatever pieces they return. ⚠ It reads what stands, never where the earlier parts stood: a part given back while a later one stood moved the rest along by a cent, and the last unit had nothing to come back on (the reader's finding 2). The last one takes the rest with no special case, and the dialog says what goes back by the same function. VAT comes out of the gross per rate, as a device does. The device comes to the same money: what it is given goes by `linesOnDevice` (§9, D2).
- **All of a sale in one go keeps its own figures.** No lines named, or every line named in full, on a sale nothing has touched: the document is the one it always was, with the sale's subtotal, tax and total, `register.storno.partial` unset, and the dispatch's lines returned as they were. Everything else is a part, `partial: true`, with its own lines.
- **What a whole request means once a part has gone: all that is left.** The state ends `full` on the claim that leaves nothing of the sale, and only that claim writes `register.cancelledAt`, which SUPTO 18.5 prints as the moment the sale was cancelled. Given back, the sale reads `partial` or `none` again, and 18.5 no longer lists it.
- **While one may have printed, no other storno of that sale goes through**, not even of a line it does not name: 409 `storno_in_doubt`, as R2's refund door does. A person is about to look at the device, and pressing on past it is how a device ends up with two. It keeps its quantities and its money, holds the close, counts nowhere, and goes through the settle door (§6). Printed runs the rest, those lines only. Not printed voids it and gives both back. A `to-invoice` meanwhile leaves its lines out: it may still come back.
- **Each claim has its own age** (`stornoClaims[].at`). `releaseOrphanClaims` pulls an id with no document by that, and a second claim no longer hides an older orphan. `cancelledAt` is read too: it is written by the claim that took the last of the sale, so past a lease it says that of every claim on it, and it is the only age a claim from before R3 has. The journal's orphan read and the session's sweep ask the same, of `partial` too. `withoutClaim` is the one update that takes a claim off, lines and money with it, for a give-back and for an orphan alike.
- **Split tender, the question the brief asked.** The operator picks in the dialog: as the sale was paid, or a method, and an API caller can name a split. Named rows are each above 0 (422 `storno_refund_not_positive`) and come to what the storno gives back (422 `storno_refund_mismatch`, with the amount), whole or in part: the drawer takes what the rows say, and the device prints the storno's own total. "As the sale was paid" is what the sale still holds per method (`leftByMethod`: what it kept, less what live stornos gave back that way), and it goes by **one method when one still holds all of it**, the first the sale names (`refundOfPart`), because a device prints one payment type on a storno for the whole amount: that is the case where the device, the drawer and the card terminal agree. Larger than any one, it draws on them in the sale's order, so the last part gives each method back the rest, and the session's buckets come to 0. ⚠ Then the device prints the first method for the whole part, as a whole storno of a split sale has always done (`refundMethod`). The session and the books have it right; the device's day does not. A pro-rata split would have made that the normal case.
- **The invoice for what is left** (the owner's decision). `to-invoice` reads the sale's budget (`soldByLine`): what it sold, less what stornos took, in doubt included. The state's lines gain `returned`, a line all of which came back takes no part, and "whole" for a sale to a named customer means whole of what is left. The invoice has its own totals, never the sale's, and its payment rows mirror what the sale still holds per method. Of an invoice and a storno at once, each reads the other after its own claim, as D-14 always had them: the storno gives everything back if a live invoice stands, and the invoice checks itself against the claims as they are then. A sale that has a live invoice still takes no storno, of any line (`cash_sale_invoiced`, FR-21 §3.5). An orphan past its lease is released before the invoice reads the sale, or it costs the invoice those lines.
- **With R2's refund, either way round.** A part first: what is left is what a refund's `leftByRate` reads, because the part wrote it there. A refund first: a whole request is refused by name (`cash_sale_refunded`, R2's test unchanged), and lines go back while they fit, per rate, or 409 `storno_exceeds_budget` with `left`, in words that name nobody: a line's own parts never come to more than the line, so it takes money gone another way, but the door does not read whose. Reaching that takes a void that raced the refund, since a live invoice refuses the storno anyway, so the normal order is part, then invoice, then note. `claimantInDoubt` and the refund door's `finishDeadClaims` read a till storno among the claims too: a dead part is finished, and a held one is named when the refund needs what it holds.
- **A line on a storno carries its discount now, whole or in part** (`printStorno`, all five drivers). It did not: a whole storno of a sale with a discount printed the undiscounted line, the driver tendered the difference in cash, and the device's storno was larger than its sale. ⚠ This is the one change to a whole storno. The terminal rings no discount, so it takes a joined back-office sale or an API caller to reach it.
- **A back-office sale prints nothing and has no drawer, so its refund may be what it was paid by** (`assertStornoTender`), a bank transfer or `other`, whole or in part, named or not. At the till a refund stays what a receipt can carry, text or fiscal, as the sale was.
- **Stock** (`partReturned`): the lines it names that left from that dispatch, at the cost that product left at (`resolvedUnitCost`), weighted over the dispatch's lines of that product, because which of two lines of one product a part returns is not something the shelf knows. A product with no stock kept moved nothing and returns nothing. A credit note's return goes at that cost too (§9, D4).
- **A fenced import brings a printed storno's stock back**, whole or in part, from the sale's dispatch here (`bringStornoBack`, `returnStornoStock`). Until this it went through `createInvoiceStockMovement`, which reads an outgoing document as a dispatch, so the hub was off by twice the quantity per storno. The export runs in `_id` order, so a sale is here ahead of its storno.
- **Lines with no id** are named by their place (`#1`): a sale copied raw from an old POS row has none, and nothing edits a till sale. `to-invoice` and an attach name them the same way, and write it into `cashSaleLineId`: read by an id they do not have they were all one line, so an invoice for all of it carried only the last line and claimed nothing, and the sale could be invoiced again. **A sale with a negative line** goes back whole only (422 `storno_not_splittable`): such a line has no share of what the receipt printed.
- **A partly stornoed sale cannot be edited or voided**, from any origin (`isImmutableRegisterDocument`). A void would reverse the whole dispatch on top of what already came back. A number typed off a slip stands once any claim is on the sale, and again once it is given back (`stornoCites`).
- **Readers need no change, as §2.5 said, and none got one**: each reads a storno at its own total and lines, a sale stays `paid` and `completed` until the state is `full`, and `bookedOnItsSession` takes the one-argument `stornoIncrements`. What did change is what asks for `full`: the door, the journal (status `partial`, its filter, its badge „частично сторнирана" and three more languages), `to-invoice`'s offer, and `canStornoJournalRow`.
- **The UI.** One dialog, `StornoDialog.vue`, opened from the journal's list and from a sale's page, in the POS and in invoicing. All of it stays the default and one press, with no field in the way. "Part of it", offered whenever there is anything to pick (`canPickPart`: one line of one unit or less counts, an article sold by the kilo goes back 0.3 of 0.8), opens the lines, what is left of each, a quantity, what goes back, and the method. It reads the sale's page when it opens; one that fails to load offers all of it, as it always did.
- **SUPTO is version 5** (`config/supto.json`; `declared` stays empty): 4 at R3 itself, and 5 for its follow-ups, `stornoClaims[].cents` and `cashSaleLineId` naming a line by its place. `stornoState` has a new value, and `register.stornoClaims` and `register.storno.partial` are new, which is what R2's `refundClaims` was a version for. No table changes shape: 18.4 lists each part with its own lines and total, citing the sale. 18.5 lists the sale once all of it is back and proved.

**Its rollback.** To R2: `partial` is not in its enum, so a save through the schema fails, but raw reads go on. It reads such a sale as not stornoed, and only `register.refundClaims`, which every part wrote, stops a whole storno of it (`cash_sale_refunded`, by its own code). `to-invoice` there invoices the whole sale, for money that has partly gone back, and a void there reverses all of its dispatch. To R1, nothing stops either. So roll back only with no partial storno printed, or fix forward. During a roll (`maxSurge: 1`) a press from the new dialog reaching the old pod is the one way to lose a part: that pod does not read `lines`, so it reverses all of it. Bounded by the length of the roll, and only a tab that loaded the new page and hit the old pod can do it.

## 9. What the prod sweep changed (2026-11-02, #461)

A sweep of all of this on prod, in the demo org, with a simulated Eltrade over the real WebSocket bridge. Nothing printed twice, and only the owner's test org prints, on a device that is not fiscalized. These had to go before a fiscalized unit sells. D8, D9 and D11 are filed apart. Where a ruling left a choice, what was chosen:

- **D1. The device decides what a till sale prints, never a switch** (`decideSaleMode`, `receiptMode`). In a fresh browser the till's „Нефискален" switch made a fiscal device print a slip: 200, money and stock booked, and the Z never saw it. Off on a device that is not fiscalized, the print `failed` with the money booked. Now the sale asks the device in its turn on it: text only when its own status says `fiscalized === false`. The switch is gone from the terminal, which keeps the chip saying what the device says. The request's `nonFiscal` is taken and never read, for old tabs. The УНП is drawn in that turn, so a slip has none, and a sale that gives way in its turn leaves no gap in that series.
  - **A Tremol and a Datecs report nothing about it, so they always print fiscally.** ⚠ A Tremol that is not fiscalized refuses that and answers `failed` on every sale, money and stock booked, where the switch let it print text. A Datecs has no text receipt either way. Reading the Tremol's status is the way to give it one.
  - **A status read that fails is never "not fiscalized".** It takes the path of a device that went away: nothing prints, the row is a fiscal one with its УНП, reading `failed`, as any print that cannot reach the device leaves, and the sale's money stands (D-4).
  - **A sale that started fiscally stays fiscal.** Beyond what was ruled: one that started as text and comes back to a device fiscalized since prints fiscally, where a receipt is held. A slip has no legal weight, the Z has to see the sale, and there is no money to hold back while a person looks.
  - **A storno stays what its sale was,** as it did: a fiscal storno of a fiscal sale, and of a text sale nothing fiscal, on whichever device.
  - **A joined back-office sale's `receipt` only asks.** Either value prints what the device says, so `text` on a fiscal device prints a fiscal receipt, and what no receipt can carry is refused on both.
  - **Who has no operator code:** the door asks the device only for them. A fiscal receipt refuses them with nothing written, and a slip does not need one. **The two-hour rule** asks every new sale, since nothing says it is text until its turn.
